# HCOS™ Evidence Review Workflow

**Document ID:** HCOS-ERW-001
**Repository:** `hcos-evidence`
**Category:** Method
**Status:** Draft v1.0

---

# Purpose

The HCOS™ Evidence Review Workflow provides a standardized process for identifying, evaluating, synthesizing, documenting, and stewarding evidence used throughout the HCOS™ ecosystem.

The workflow promotes scientific rigor while recognizing that understanding human systems requires integrating multiple forms of evidence, including research, historical records, organizational knowledge, and lived experience.

Its purpose is not simply to collect citations.

Its purpose is to improve understanding while maintaining transparency regarding what is known, what is inferred, and what remains uncertain.

---

# Guiding Principles

Every evidence review should:

* protect human dignity;
* prioritize accuracy over certainty;
* distinguish evidence from interpretation;
* preserve uncertainty where appropriate;
* document evidence boundaries;
* support organizational learning;
* maintain source transparency;
* and continuously improve as new evidence becomes available.

Evidence stewardship is an ongoing process rather than a one-time activity.

# Step 1 — Define the Question

Clearly define:

* the topic;
* intended audience;
* purpose;
* system being evaluated;
* and intended use.

Questions should be specific enough to guide an efficient and transparent evidence review.

Examples:

* What evidence supports this HCOS™ principle?
* What implementation science informs this method?
* What human factors literature explains this workflow?
* What historical evidence supports this cultural analysis?

---

# Step 2 — Define the Scope

Determine:

* population;
* setting;
* time period;
* discipline;
* geographical context;
* organizational context;
* and evidence needed.

Document any exclusions before beginning the review.

---

# Step 3 — Search for Evidence

Search appropriate sources, such as:

* PubMed
* MEDLINE
* CINAHL
* PsycINFO
* ERIC
* Web of Science
* Scopus
* Government publications
* Professional society guidance
* Historical archives
* Legal records
* Organizational documents
* Community-based sources

For narrative analyses, include relevant cultural and historical sources as described in HCOS-NES-001.

---

# Step 4 — Appraise Evidence

Evaluate each source for:

* relevance;
* methodological quality;
* credibility;
* applicability;
* limitations;
* conflicts of interest;
* publication date;
* and population or setting similarity.

Evidence should be assessed based on its ability to answer the review question rather than prestige alone.

---

# Step 5 — Classify Evidence

Classify evidence into appropriate categories.

## Scientific Evidence

* Systematic reviews
* Meta-analyses
* Randomized trials
* Observational studies
* Qualitative research
* Human factors research
* Implementation science
* Organizational science

## Historical Evidence

* Official records
* Archival documents
* Biographies
* Historical scholarship

## Cultural Evidence

* Interviews
* Artistic works
* Speeches
* Community narratives
* First-person accounts

## Organizational Evidence

* Policies
* Internal reports
* Workflow documentation
* Operational data
* Quality improvement findings

## HCOS™ Internal Evidence

* HCOS™ standards
* HCOS™ methods
* HCOS™ architectures
* HCOS™ disciplines
* HCOS™ instruments

---

# Step 6 — Identify Evidence Boundaries

For every major claim, determine whether it represents:

* established scientific evidence;
* historical evidence;
* organizational evidence;
* cultural evidence;
* authoritative guidance;
* HCOS™ synthesis;
* expert interpretation;
* hypothesis;
* or future research.

Avoid presenting HCOS™ synthesis as independently validated unless such validation exists.

---

# Step 7 — Develop HCOS™ Systems Synthesis

Evidence should not simply be summarized.

It should be integrated into a human-centered systems interpretation.

Ask questions such as:

* What system pressures are present?
* What protections exist?
* Where does burden accumulate?
* What interactions exist across systems?
* What assumptions are visible?
* What assumptions remain hidden?
* What practical lessons emerge?
* What opportunities exist for stewardship and redesign?

Clearly distinguish:

* documented findings;
* systems interpretation;
* and original HCOS™ contributions.

---

# Step 8 — Citation and Documentation

Apply HCOS-CIT-001.

Ensure:

* APA 7 in-text citations;
* complete bibliography;
* evidence notes;
* source transparency;
* version history;
* and repository documentation.

Every factual claim should be traceable to an appropriate source.

---

# Step 9 — Human Review

Before publication:

* verify factual accuracy;
* review interpretations;
* evaluate evidence boundaries;
* identify unsupported claims;
* confirm appropriate citation;
* and assess clarity for the intended audience.

Where uncertainty exists, preserve it rather than overstating conclusions.

---

# Step 10 — Publication and Stewardship

Publication is not the end of the evidence lifecycle.

Continue to:

* monitor new research;
* update references;
* revise interpretations when appropriate;
* document substantive changes;
* and maintain transparent version history.

HCOS™ treats evidence stewardship as a continuous learning process.

# Stewardship Principle

> **Evidence is not collected simply to support conclusions.**
>
> **Evidence is stewarded to improve understanding.**
>
> **Human stories reveal lived experience.**
>
> **Context explains surrounding conditions.**
>
> **Research identifies recurring patterns.**
>
> **HCOS™ integrates these perspectives into practical guidance for healthier human systems while remaining transparent about what is known, what is inferred, and what remains uncertain.**
